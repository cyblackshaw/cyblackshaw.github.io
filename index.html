PhD Candidate · Hurricane Hazards and Risk Analysis Group

I study tropical cyclone and multi-hazard risk under a changing climate, with a focus on extreme winds, coastal flooding, compound urban hazards, and infrastructure resilience.

My research combines physical hazard and climate modeling with uncertainty quantification and risk analysis. I also develop interactive scientific tools that make evolving hazard estimates and climate-sensitive design information easier to explore and communicate.

Research focus

How do tropical cyclone hazards change across forecasts and future climates, how does that uncertainty propagate into impacts and engineering decisions, and how can those risks be communicated for more resilient communities and infrastructure?

Research

Hazards, uncertainty, and resilience across timescales

My current work spans real-time forecasting, long-term climate risk, and compound urban hazards. Across these projects, I connect meteorological and climate information to hazard estimates, infrastructure impacts, and decision-relevant tools.

01 · Real-time risk

Tropical Cyclone Multi-Hazard Forecasting

I integrate official tropical cyclone forecasts with wind, rainfall, storm-tide, and power-outage models for real-time multi-hazard assessment.

The research evaluates how uncertainty propagates from meteorological forecasts into hazard estimates and tracks how those estimates evolve across forecast updates. An interactive platform makes those changing hazards directly explorable.

Focus: forecast uncertainty · wind, rainfall & storm tide · power outages · risk communication

02 · Future climate

Structural Design Winds Under Future Climates

I evaluate hurricane wind hazards and structural design codes across CMIP6 climate-model ensembles to understand how climate uncertainty translates into design-wind estimates and structural implications.

The work finds substantial variability across projected wind hazards and uses an interactive calculator to compare design winds by location, building characteristics, and climate future.

Focus: extreme winds · CMIP6 ensembles · structural design · climate uncertainty

03 · Compound hazards

Compound Tropical Cyclone Hazards in Cities

I couple urban-canopy, atmospheric, and inundation models to characterize tropical-cyclone flooding and winds under extreme-heat conditions in urban environments.

This framework enables city-block-scale assessment of post-storm heat stress and hurricane–heat interactions, helping identify risks that are missed when hazards are considered independently.

Focus: urban climate · flood modeling · post-storm heat · compound risk

Connected work

From hazard characterization to infrastructure impacts

Related research includes generating tropical cyclone and wind-hazard inputs for New York City energy-resilience modeling; integrating paleohurricane reconstructions with hydrodynamic storm-surge modeling; developing wind-driven synthetic hurricane-track methods; and modeling current and future hurricane flooding and damage in the New York City subway system.

Interactive research

Background

Research, teaching & leadership

I am a PhD candidate in Civil and Environmental Engineering at Princeton University, advised by Professor Ning Lin in the Hurricane Hazards and Risk Analysis Group. I received my M.A. from Princeton in 2023 and my B.S. in Civil Engineering, with a minor in Environmental Engineering, from Columbia University in 2021.

I am an NSF Graduate Research Fellow. Alongside my research, I have taught risk analysis and hydrology, mentored undergraduate and high-school researchers, and led interdisciplinary climate research through the Princeton Environment and Climate Scholars program.
