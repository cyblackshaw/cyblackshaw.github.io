Research

Three connected questions

Forecast cones · tracks · county impacts

Hurricane Forecasting & Uncertainty

I develop forecasting workflows that combine official NHC forecasts, cone-edge tracks, and dynamical model tracks to estimate county-level wind, rainfall, storm tide, and power outages.

The work asks how uncertainty in track, intensity, and storm size propagates into hazard and impact estimates across successive advisories.

Design wind maps · CMIP6 · return periods

Design Winds Under Climate Change

I estimate tropical cyclone design wind speeds for U.S. coastal counties using synthetic storms, reanalysis, and CMIP6 climate projections.

I compare stationary and nonstationary approaches and examine how structural design winds may evolve through 2100 across emissions scenarios and risk categories.

Urban heat · inundation · coupled hazards

Compound Urban Hazards

I use atmospheric and urban-canopy modeling to study interactions between tropical cyclones and heatwaves, including post-storm nighttime heat stress.

This work links flood and urban-climate modeling to understand compound exposure and infrastructure consequences in cities.

About

Christine Ye Shu Blackshaw

I am a PhD researcher in Civil and Environmental Engineering at Princeton University, advised by Professor Ning Lin. My research sits at the intersection of tropical cyclone science, climate change, hazard modeling, and infrastructure risk.

I work across Python, MATLAB, R, GIS, high-performance computing, climate-model ensembles, and numerical hazard models including PepC, ADCIRC, SFINCS, WRF, PUCM, and HEC-RAS.
