I study tropical cyclone hazards and infrastructure risk, with a focus on how forecast uncertainty and climate change affect extreme winds, compound hazards, and decisions about resilient infrastructure.

01

Tropical Cyclone Forecasting & Uncertainty

I am developing a forecasting platform that uses official National Hurricane Center forecasts, cone-edge tracks, and dynamical model tracks to estimate county-level wind, rainfall, storm tide, and power outages.

The project examines how uncertainty in storm track, intensity, and size propagates into hazard and impact estimates, and how well forecast hazard ranges contain hazards modeled from best-track storm inputs across successive advisories.

Methods: tropical cyclone hazard modeling, forecast ensembles, uncertainty propagation, geospatial analysis, outage modeling

Design-wind maps / return periods Suggested image: historical vs. future design-wind map

02

Design Winds Under Climate Change

I estimate tropical cyclone design wind speeds for U.S. coastal counties from Texas to Maine using synthetic tropical cyclones, ERA5 reanalysis, and CMIP6 climate projections under multiple emissions scenarios.

This work compares stationary and nonstationary approaches to extreme-wind estimation, benchmarks historical estimates against ASCE 7-22, and assesses how design winds may change through 2100 across structural risk categories.

Methods: extreme-value and return-period analysis, climate-model ensembles, nonstationary hazard analysis, synthetic storm modeling, HPC

Urban heat / inundation / compound hazards Suggested image: WRF temperature or flood-exposure map

03

Compound Urban Flood & Heat Hazards

I use atmospheric and urban-canopy modeling to investigate interactions between tropical cyclones and heatwaves, including post-storm nighttime heat stress in urban environments.

The broader goal is to integrate flood and urban-climate modeling to evaluate compound exposure to inundation and heat and better understand the risks that emerge when hazards occur in close succession.

Methods: WRF, urban-canopy modeling, flood modeling, compound-hazard analysis, urban climate

Additional research

My related work includes storm-surge risk assessment using hydrodynamic simulations and paleohurricane records; coastal flooding and power-system resilience under climate change; and earlier research on New York City subway flood propagation, damage, and hurricane flood protection.

About

Background

I am a PhD candidate in Civil and Environmental Engineering at Princeton University, advised by Professor Ning Lin. I received my MA from Princeton in 2023 and my BS in Civil Engineering, with a minor in Environmental Engineering, from Columbia University in 2021.

I am an NSF Graduate Research Fellow and have also worked in teaching, mentoring, and campus climate research through Princeton's environmental programs.
